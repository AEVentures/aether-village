# Aether Village — Preliminary Business Plan

## Executive summary
Aether Village is a proposed founder residency, AI lab, retreat venue, and—only after land-use approval—modular hospitality village at 26480 SW Wilken Ln near West Linn, Oregon. The 68.48-acre estate is currently marketed around $5.4M–$5.65M and includes a 13,023 sq. ft. residence, 10 bedrooms, 12 baths, a connected four-bedroom guest house, extensive gathering areas, and an approved event permit.

The strategy is phased. Phase 1 operates only uses already legal and insurable at closing: owner occupancy, room/residency agreements where permitted, founder retreats, and events within the existing permit. Phase 2 pursues county land-use approval, sanitation, access, fire, building-code, utility, and hospitality approvals for a small pilot of modular capsule lodging. Expansion occurs only after legal operation and demonstrated unit economics.

This is a feasibility-stage concept, not an offering to invest, a promise that capsules can be placed on the land, or a claim that projected returns are assured.

## Mission
Create a high-trust place where founders, engineers, artists, researchers, and operators live and work near nature while building practical AI products that benefit people.

## Property thesis
- 68.48 acres within approximately 30 minutes of downtown Portland.
- 10-bedroom, 12-bath main residence and connected four-bedroom guest house.
- 13,023 sq. ft. of existing residential and gathering space.
- Event-oriented grounds, multiple gathering areas, large garages, and an approved event permit per the listing.
- Strong visual identity suitable for premium retreats and founder residencies.

## Critical constraint: land use
Public listing data identifies the parcel as Clackamas County EFU (Exclusive Farm Use), tax lot 31E08 00200. EFU land is protected for agricultural use. A dense residential or short-term-rental capsule village should not be treated as allowed. Before contracting without a broad feasibility contingency, obtain a written pre-application assessment from Clackamas County Planning and Zoning covering:

1. Existing dwelling and guest-house occupancy rights.
2. Scope and transferability of the event permit.
3. Room rentals, co-living, retreats, conferences, and short-term lodging.
4. Whether any modular unit can qualify under Oregon structural specialty code.
5. Conditional-use, campground, farm-stay, agri-tourism, or destination-resort pathways.
6. Maximum unit count, setbacks, wildfire standards, emergency access, parking, and ADA requirements.
7. Septic capacity, water rights/well capacity, stormwater, power, and broadband.
8. Whether a zone change or exception would be required.

The acquisition offer should include land-use, financing, appraisal, environmental, title, insurance, structural, well, septic, and event-permit contingencies.

## Capsule procurement reality
Capsule Castle advertises quote-based modular units, with public editorial pricing generally ranging from roughly $12,000–$40,000 and a starting claim around $18,630. The manufacturer states that freight is separate, on-site installation is not included, and utilities/site connections must be completed locally. A $20,000 shell assumption is not an installed cost.

For underwriting, use $75,000–$150,000 per legal, operational capsule until vendor quotes and civil/site designs are obtained. That allowance includes unit, ocean freight, duty, inland transport, crane, foundation, utility trenching, electrical/plumbing/HVAC adaptation, code engineering, permits, furnishings, landscaping, and contingency. Confirm Oregon certification, snow/wind/seismic design, flame spread, egress, energy code, accessibility, warranty, replacement parts, and lender/insurer acceptance before ordering.

## Product
### Phase 0 — diligence and demand validation (0–120 days)
- Secure property control only with protective contingencies.
- Obtain zoning and permit opinions.
- Verify room count, legal occupancy, fire/life safety, septic and water capacity.
- Collect non-binding resident, retreat, and partner applications.
- Obtain lender term sheets and third-party market study.

### Phase 1 — estate residency and retreats (months 4–12)
- Open 6–8 legally rentable rooms, preserving owner/manager space and common areas.
- Run curated founder cohorts and company retreats.
- Establish community charter, quiet hours, guest rules, security, and insurance.
- Avoid capsule expenditure until entitlements are clear.

### Phase 2 — capsule pilot (months 12–24)
- Install 3–6 code-compliant units only after final approvals.
- Measure occupancy, ADR, maintenance, utility loads, and resident satisfaction for 12 months.
- Expand only when trailing results meet lender-approved thresholds.

### Phase 3 — measured expansion (year 3+)
- Add units in small tranches within approved limits.
- Introduce memberships, sponsored residencies, education programs, and corporate partnerships.

## Base operating model — existing estate only
Illustrative assumptions pending market study and legal review:

- 8 rentable rooms at $3,250/month average, 85% occupancy: $265,200/year.
- 18 retreats/events at $15,000 average venue/program revenue: $270,000/year.
- Memberships/workshops/sponsorships: $120,000/year.
- Total gross revenue: $655,200/year.
- Operating expenses before debt (55%): $360,360/year.
- Net operating income before debt: $294,840/year.

At an illustrative $4.16M loan (75% of $5.55M) amortized over 30 years at 7.25%, annual principal and interest is approximately $340,000, before property taxes and any lender reserves. The base estate operation therefore does not safely cover acquisition debt on these assumptions. The deal requires one or more of: a lower price, substantially more equity, seller financing, higher verified retreat revenue, approved capsule income, or an outside operating business capable of supporting debt.

## Key risks and mitigations
- **Zoning denial:** contingent acquisition; estate-only operating fallback; no capsule orders before approval.
- **Debt-service gap:** larger equity tranche, price reduction, seller financing, verified contracted retreat revenue.
- **Imported-unit code failure:** Oregon engineer review and authority-having-jurisdiction confirmation before purchase.
- **Infrastructure cost overrun:** civil design, utility study, fixed bids, 20–30% contingency.
- **Seasonality:** medium-term residencies plus corporate programs.
- **Community conflict:** charter, professional management, enforceable conduct policy, grievance process.
- **Securities risk:** do not solicit passive investment through the resident application; use securities counsel for any capital raise.
- **Fair housing/privacy:** counsel-reviewed criteria, data minimization, retention limits, protected-class neutrality.
- **Brand/property rights:** use original imagery or licensed listing assets only.

### Days 91–120
- Investment committee go/no-go.

## Go/no-go gates
Proceed only if:
- Counsel identifies a viable legal path for Phase 1.
- Appraisal and inspection support the basis.
- Capital stack includes at least 12 months of debt and operating reserves.
- Underwritten DSCR is at least 1.25x on contracted/defensible revenue—not capsule speculation.
- County and code officials provide a plausible written capsule pathway before capsule capital is committed.
